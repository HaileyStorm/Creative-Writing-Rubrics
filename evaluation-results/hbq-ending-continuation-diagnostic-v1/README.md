# Scene exits and false hooks: synthetic design diagnostic

Two CWR questions ask whether a passage avoids substituting arbitrary
cliffhangers or withheld basics for earned momentum, and whether a scene exit
places emphasis effectively and relates appropriately to what follows. A
previous synthetic draft without a following passage made the second question
hard to assess and produced identical provisional answers for both questions.
This [four-case draft](cases.md) supplies a scene and a following passage in
every case to explore the boundary between those questions.

The current assistant authored the synthetic cases; its exact effective
generation settings were not independently captured. Two agents reviewed the
same packet without its separate design note. The first had seen the previous
ending draft and discussion of criterion overlap; the second had not. The
packet's repeated scenes make its paired structure visible. Both agents gave
the following provisional answers; several answers were explicitly qualified.

| Case | Avoids arbitrary hook | Effective exit relation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | NO | YES |
| 02 | YES | YES |
| 03 | YES | YES |
| 04 | YES | NO |

The reviewers said case 01 could deliberately delay information, and case 04's
apparently incidental notice could express emotional avoidance. Both readings
are plausible; the table is not an answer key. Cases 01 and 02 change disclosure,
motivation and later evidence together. Cases 03 and 04 keep the scene ending
identical and change the following passage, so that contrast concerns the
relationship across the boundary, not exit wording alone. The fresh reviewer
also observed that the exit question combines final emphasis and its relation
to what follows; those components can point in different directions.

This is a small, post-design agent critique of synthetic material. It is not a
frozen study, human rating, model collection, reliability estimate, evidence of
human alignment or a reason to change weights or promote a rubric. Reviewers
shared the same four texts and their agreement does not establish independent
human validation. Short contexts, repeated structure and conspicuous contrasts
limit transfer to real writing. Further development should separate the exit
components and include less obvious, diverse cases before prospective testing.
The cases are now public development material and are not an unused holdout.
