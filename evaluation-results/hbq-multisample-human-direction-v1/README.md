# Historical human-reference scalar directions

This retrospective secondary diagnostic uses the completed, already-opened
HANNA development repeat panel. It adds correct/reversed direction accounting
to the existing rank association, repeat variation and model-tie diagnostics.
The profile is frozen before this diagnostic runs, after the original data
collection; it is not a precollection preregistration. One provider-free execution
completed all72rows and900derived comparisons. An independent readback checked
every row, all360prompt-cluster omissions and their sensitivity ranges.

All15original and10later human-aggregate pairs have strict saved directions.
The later compact/holistic means therefore provide zero usable directions, while
the later HBQ mean reverses eight reference directions. These finite-panel
findings distinguish compression from reversed ordering. They do not identify
a general quality winner, establish a causal rubric-version effect, or supply
unused confirmation. Only one later pair shares an originating prompt; none do
in the original cohort. All repetition views and sensitivity ranges remain in
the private aggregate report, SHA256
`f73b1dfa9f94bc352dfbb3a29aae8262f108cb0250cf6d93ebbdad2dbea8e73f`.

The original six-item and later five-item HBQ cohorts remain separate. Identical
cohort membership applies to all six arms, with all 15/10 unordered item pairs
retained. Five repetition views and one five-repeat-mean view produce 72 rows
and 900 derived pair comparisons. These comparisons are not new votes. No
outcome-based pair filtering, pooled primary HBQ estimate or version-effect
claim is made.

Every pair uses a stable lexical ordering of its fixed private item identities.
The three-by-three table records the signs of left-minus-right human and model
scores. Correct and reversed directions require a strict human aggregate
direction and a strict model direction. A model tie on a strict-reference pair
is retained separately. Human-aggregate ties are split into model ties and model
directions; they never count as correct or establish human indifference.

Correct/wrong rates divide by all strict-reference pairs, including model ties.
Directional coverage is reported both on strict-reference pairs and on all
planned pairs. Every denominator and missing count is explicit. Zero-denominator
rates are null. Same-origin-prompt and across-origin-prompt tables remain separate;
the full cohorts contain zero and one same-prompt pairs respectively.

The human reference is the existing published `human_overall` composite: six
dimension means, each from three HANNA ratings. This is a scalar-order proxy,
not observed human pairwise preference ballots. Most comparisons cross prompts.
The historical compact/holistic speculative-fiction applicability mismatch
remains a limitation; this diagnostic does not repair it or identify a quality
winner. Items and HBQ versions differ jointly across cohorts.

Leave-one-prompt-cluster-out sensitivity omits the same cluster from every arm
within each cohort. Each omission retains all remaining pairs, recalculates
denominators and reports rate ranges and undefined cases. Public omission
ordinals share a fixed opaque prompt-hash order across arms. These are sensitivity
ranges, not confidence intervals. Pairs share items/prompts; no pair bootstrap or
independent-pair inference is performed. Repetition indices are logical schedule
strata, not independently sampled human judges.

The pinned floor helper supplies the existing scalar preflight and Decimal/
Fraction arithmetic. Its floor calculation, original scoring and native replay
are not run. The additional lexical projection reads only item/prompt bindings
and human aggregates, checks agreement across all arms and against the pinned
frozen sample aggregate, and skips prose, responses, raw ratings, quality bands
and cutpoints. Input bytes are rechecked before exclusively creating the output.
The report contains aggregate counts and commitments, without private item IDs,
scores, human targets, prompt hashes or absolute locators. Saved native/source
claims are inherited, not freshly qualified. Historical tool-disabled declarations
do not certify past tool removal, provider identity or contact cardinality.
