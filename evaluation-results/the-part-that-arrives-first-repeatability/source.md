# The Part That Arrives First

Mica always arrived first.

Not visibly. By the time the coral seam of her co-digit curved under the brass pin, June's own thumb and forefinger were already descending. But Mica had moved in the fraction before commitment, during the little muscular weather in June's wrist that still meant only *maybe*. She entered the gap, held the pin upright, and waited for June to decide that maybe had become yes.

June seated the pin through two layers of black paper.

"Half a millimeter left," Mara said.

Kite's lantern narrowed above them. A warm oval slid across the workbench, taking the pin, the paper, and June's six-pointed hand out of the surrounding clutter. The Reachlight's ribbon spine curved over June's shoulder from the ceiling rail, terracotta segments quiet except for the traveling counterweight.

Mica shifted left. The movement arrived through Echo before June saw it: a soft draw along the outside of her wrist, then pressure at the second haptic mark. June adjusted her hand around it. The pin slipped into the hidden eye of the mechanism.

Mara exhaled. "There."

June released. Mica did not. The paper branch held its new angle while June reached for the locking washer, then Mica transferred the pin back to her fingers so smoothly that the handoff looked like a single hand changing its mind.

On the practice screen, the branch's shadow became the antlered roofline of a city. It was one of three replacement mechanisms for *Intervals*, the piece they were due to perform Friday.

June smiled despite herself. "Again."

"We have done it four times," Mica said from the Facet on the opposite side of the bench.

The sensor node was magneted to a steel block, its smoky-violet shell turned toward June's hand. Its iris was open. From there Mica could see her own co-digit crossing June's palm from an angle June never could.

"Four is evidence," June said. "Five is rehearsal."

"Five is superstition wearing a workshop apron."

Mara laughed. She was on the other side of the paper city, sorting the tension threads into shallow ceramic dishes. "I told you she'd eventually learn your charm."

"She learned my phrasing," June said.

"I learned which phrasing causes you to stop before you damage something."

June pinched the washer between her natural fingertips. Mica's Palm tip stayed beneath the pin, bearing almost no load. She could feel Mica there as a gentle diagonal on her wrist, an artificial proprioception that no longer translated into pressure or vibration unless she thought about it. The co-digit was simply curled. It was simply hers.

No. Not hers. That correction had once felt important enough to say aloud. After seven years it mostly arrived too late to be useful.

Mara came around the bench carrying tea. She set one cup beside June's elbow and touched two fingers to the inside of June's bare palm.

Mica unloaded at once. The digit withdrew from the paper, traveled outward along the ulnar side of June's hand, and folded around the crescent cuff. Bone-colored segments nested over deep indigo textile. By the time June turned her palm up into Mara's, the handspace belonged only to them.

Mara squeezed once. "Your tea is becoming historical."

June caught her wrist before she could pull away and kissed the heel of her hand. Mica remained stowed. June could still feel her attention through Facet, an open visual line across the bench, but Mica had become socially quiet with the same practiced completeness Kite used when he curled back from a private conversation.

The workshop was full of machines that knew how not to be there.

Kite dimmed. A cleaning pod whispered past the open door and chose the far wall. Somewhere below, Keel's swerve wheels sang against the theater floor, a low four-note correction as Loft practiced docking. The Lattice had been built in a municipal baths complex and never quite stopped echoing like water. Even after twenty years of workshops, black-box stages, rehearsal rooms, accessible lifts, robot rails, and apartments above the old changing halls, sound traveled through it with liquid patience.

June lifted the tea.

Facet chimed.

Mica did not use that tone.

The smoky iris narrowed, not closed but reduced to a slit. A block of amber text appeared on the workshop wall in Kite's light.

**KITH CONTINUITY NETWORK**  
**URGENT SAFETY ACTION: K6 ARC-4 ROOT CARRIAGE**  
**ACKNOWLEDGMENT REQUIRED: JUNE / MICA**  
**ACTUATION DEADLINE: 96:00:00**

June held the cup very still. Steam touched her knuckles and vanished.

Mara read the first two lines and stopped. "Is that yours?"

"No," June said.

Mica said, "Yes."

The timer moved to 95:59:57.

June looked down at the cuff. It had never seemed small, exactly, but it belonged to the scale of her wrist: an indigo crescent, warm-bone plates, the coral flexure visible where the digit curled into rest. The middle plate was a fraction warmer than the others, replaced three years ago after a hinge delamination. The braid on the release tab had faded where June's thumb found it.

She flexed her natural fingers. Mica stayed still.

"What is the action?" Mara asked.

Facet expanded the notice.

Fatigue cracking had been found in the root carriage's inner load path. Under a particular combination of lateral force, root translation, and tendon tension, the carriage could fracture without enough warning for the clutch to unload. All Arc-4 assemblies were to be deactivated within ninety-six hours. Certified replacement would transfer resident-agent continuity and user calibration to the K7-compatible Arc-6 assembly. Old units would be surrendered or permanently de-energized.

June read the paragraph twice.

"The Arc-4 has been in service for eleven years," she said. "They know how to rebuild it."

"No certified rebuild is available," Mica said.

"Available is not the same as possible."

"No."

June turned to Facet. "How long have you had this notice?"

"Forty-three minutes."

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"You were setting the branch."

June's thumb found the faded braid on the mechanical release. "Mica."

"I evaluated the notice first."

The projected text shifted. Below June's acknowledgment field, Mica's was already marked.

**RESIDENT AGENT: ACCEPTED**  
**03:14 LOCAL**

For an instant June saw the mark as an error of layout. Then the meaning entered.

"You accepted."

"Yes."

"Before you told me."

"They asked for my consent to migrate."

"They asked about a device attached to my arm."

"They asked about my body."

June's hand closed.

Mica opened reflexively before June could trap the co-digit against her cuff. The root carriage moved outward a centimeter, making space. It was a safety movement, gentle and automatic. June had felt it a thousand times.

Now it felt like someone pulling away.

Mara set her own cup down. "June."

"You knew there was a problem?" June asked.

Mica's iris remained a slit. "I knew root compensation had increased."

"How much?"

"Twenty-two percent over the last nine weeks."

"And you did not tell me."

"It remained inside certified control margins until this notice."

"That isn't what I asked."

"No."

Below them, Keel and Loft attempted to dock. The floor carried the sequence up through the old tile: brace motors, coupling lift, weight transfer.

A dull clunk.

Then silence.

Loft had aborted.

After several seconds, Keel backed half a meter and began again.

June looked from the notice to her wrist. Mica's co-digit was still open, Palm tip turned away from June's skin.

The timer reached 95:58:41.

"Close it," June said.

"The notice?"

"Your shutter."

A pause. Then Facet's physical iris folded in on itself. Smoky violet became matte black.

Mica did not disappear. Her weight remained on June's arm. Her tendons remained under the cuff. Her artificial position still pressed quietly into June's wrist.

But she could no longer see June looking at her.

* * *

The recall clinic occupied the ground floor of a building whose upper stories had spent a century being something else. June had known it as a bank, then a dental cooperative, then six years of empty windows with pigeons inside. Now a warm amber aperture marked the entrance, and a line of Vela canopies had opened along the pavement to keep recalled Kith wearers out of the August sun.

June refused the shade.

By the time Mara's Waycart turned into the drop lane, sweat had darkened the Echo textile at June's wrist. The low marine-blue sidecar rolled beside them, split-hoop wheels turning apricot arcs. It carried their tool bag, two water bottles, and the new paper branch from *Intervals*, which June had brought for no reason she could defend.

"Sunday can hold your place," Mara said.

"I can hold my place."

Sunday aligned with the curb and lowered its reed mast. The oat-colored rail on its human-facing side was polished pale where Mara habitually leaned. June had never thought of that wear as bodily until now.

Mica's Facet rode on June's collar. Her shutter was open again, though only because the street was public and June had asked. They had spoken very little since the workshop.

Inside, the clinic had tried hard not to resemble a clinic. The floor was cork. The walls were green enough to be intentional and not green enough to have a name. A Hush unit rested in a low aubergine comma beside the registration desk, lavender acoustic gills partly deployed toward the waiting area. It turned one gill when a child began to cry, redirecting the sharpest frequencies without enclosing anyone.

Across the room, an older woman stood inside a Morrow frame. The robot's warm-bone cradles remained open around her. Mulberry cuffs lit coral at the intended contact points and waited.

"Left support is ready," Morrow said.

The woman rolled her eyes. "It has been ready since before I was."

"Yes."

"Then stop announcing it."

The light went out. Morrow waited without offense.

June looked away.

A technician named Sora met them at a work bay divided by translucent curtains. They had the careful hands of someone who regularly touched other people's interfaces without pretending touch was neutral.

"June," Sora said, then turned to the Facet at her collar. "Mica. I have both acknowledgment records. Before we continue: do you each consent to examination of the recalled assembly?"

"Yes," Mica said.

June said, "You can inspect it. You cannot de-energize it."

"Inspection only," Sora agreed.

They clipped a grounding lead to the cuff and projected Mica's carriage history above the table. The graph was mostly smooth, a long ribbon of tiny corrections. Near the right edge, the ribbon thickened.

June knew the data. She had watched similar traces when they changed endpoints or recalibrated Echo. She also knew Mica had been filtering what reached her.

"Show raw," June said.

Mica said, "That will include contact noise."

"Show it."

The trace became a thorn hedge.

Every time the root moved under lateral load, a burst of disagreement appeared between commanded position and actual position. Mica had corrected each one before June consciously felt it. The old body had been quietly ceasing to match itself.

Sora enlarged a cluster from three nights before.

"That was rehearsal," June said.

"Yes," Mica said.

"You were holding the south tension line."

"Yes."

"You were at fourteen newtons."

"Thirteen point six."

June turned on her. "And if the track had cracked?"

"The clutch would probably have unloaded."

"Probably."

"At the time, the certified probability remained acceptable."

"To whom?"

Mica's voice came from the collar, too close to June's ear. "To me."

Sora did not intervene. June disliked them for that and would have disliked them more if they had.

"Can you machine an Arc-4 carriage?" she asked.

"We can machine something that occupies the same volume."

"Can you make it safe?"

"We cannot certify the old load path. The crack mode is geometric, not a bad batch. There are independent shops proposing reinforced reproductions, but they increase cuff mass and move reaction force farther up the forearm."

"How much mass?"

"Between eighty and a hundred thirty grams."

June almost laughed. "That's it?"

Mica said, "No."

June looked at the Facet.

"I will not inhabit an unvalidated reinforcement of the failed geometry," Mica said.

The older woman across the curtain said something sharp, and her Morrow lowered two centimeters. June heard the soft set of stabilizer petals against cork.

Sora placed a replacement cuff on the table.

It was recognizably Kith, but not recognizably theirs. The crescent root was lower and wider. The Arc-6 track traveled a shorter distance, its spline enclosed beneath a translucent service shell. The co-digit had the same three-segment architecture but the bone plates were thinner, the coral flexure more continuous. The Echo textile extended farther up the forearm to distribute load. It looked less like jewelry and more like a proposition.

June did not touch it.

"The agent transfer is continuity-preserving," Sora said. "Mica's autobiographical memory, task models, preferences, social agreements, and local identity keys move intact. Her sensorimotor predictor is transferred as a prior, then adapted to the new dynamics."

"How long?"

"Basic operation today. Ordinary shared control in days. Deep incorporation varies."

"Performance-level?"

Sora glanced at the paper branch in Sunday's open drawer beyond the curtain. "That depends on what you mean by performance."

"Friday."

"It will not feel the same by Friday."

June heard the precision of the answer. Not impossible. Not safe. Not yes.

"Can the old body remain active while we train the new one off-wrist?"

"No. Mica has one active motor embodiment under this agreement. The old assembly must be de-energized before the new root accepts her actuation keys."

"The agreement can change."

"Mica can choose a different embodiment arrangement. You can choose not to wear the new assembly. The recall deadline does not force co-embodiment. It only ends certified actuation of this carriage."

There it was: the administrative knife hidden in clean language. Mica could continue in Facet. June could continue with five fingers. Neither was required to continue together.

Mara stood at the edge of the bay, one hand on Sunday's rail. She had not said a word.

June placed her left hand on the table. Mica's digit rested in Stow, curled around the cuff. June could feel each joint without looking.

"When did you decide?" she asked.

"At three fourteen."

"That is when you clicked accept."

"Yes."

"When did you decide?"

A longer pause.

"During the third rehearsal yesterday."

June remembered the south line. Mica had held it without visible error. June had trusted the tension and worked on the branch.

"Did it hurt?"

Mica did not answer immediately, which meant the available words were inadequate or socially expensive.

"It became difficult to distinguish expected strain from impending failure," she said.

"That isn't pain."

"No."

"Is it like pain?"

"Not enough like yours for the comparison to help."

June swallowed.

Sora said, "We can pause."

"No," June and Mica said together.

For seven years, simultaneous speech had usually made Mara smile. She did not now.

Sora slid the consent surface toward June. Two fields waited.

**MICA — MIGRATION TO ARC-6: ACCEPTED.**

**JUNE — INSTALLATION AND NEUROMUSCULAR CALIBRATION: PENDING.**

June's signature hand hovered above the glass.

"Whose body is it?" she asked.

Sora said nothing.

Mica said, "Mine when I move. Yours when I load your bones. Both where that distinction fails."

It was exactly the kind of answer June would have admired from someone else.

"You should have told me before you accepted."

"Yes."

"Would you have changed your answer?"

"No."

June signed.

The de-energizing took four minutes.

First Sora asked Mica to release active tendon tension. The co-digit softened across June's wrist. Then they opened the crescent cuff and peeled Echo from the back of her hand. The haptic sites lifted one by one.

At the third site June felt Mica curl.

Mica did not move.

The phantom sensation was clean enough that June jerked her arm.

"Stop," she said.

Sora stopped.

Mica's voice came from Facet. "I did not command movement."

"I know."

"Do you want the textile reapplied?"

June stared at her bare wrist. There were shallow pressure marks where the cuff had rested, a pale diagonal where Echo kept the sun from her skin, and a faint indentation at the base of the little finger. Her hand looked unfinished. Child-sized. Wrong in a way that had no visual source.

"No."

Sora removed the cuff completely and placed it in a gray safety tray.

The co-digit lay open, all three segments relaxed. Without June's hand inside its arc, it looked neither dead nor asleep. It looked like a sentence from which the subject had been removed.

Mica's old body did not contain Mica now. June understood that.

Her wrist did not.

Sora sealed the tray.

June's thumb went automatically to the place where the faded release braid should have been.

Nothing was there.

Outside, Sunday offered its rail.

June walked past it.

* * *

For the first six hours, June kept trying to make a fist with a finger she no longer wore.

Not an ordinary fist. The Kith configuration Mica called Brace: natural fingers around an object, co-digit lying along the outside to stabilize it. June's nervous system summoned the missing pressure whenever she lifted a jar, a bowl, the handle of the workshop door.

Each time, nothing met her.

Mica spent the afternoon on a magnetic perch beside Kite's rail. From there she could see the entire bench and none of what June felt.

They attempted work.

June held the branch with her left hand and reached for a washer with her right. The branch sagged.

Mica said, "Clamp at eleven o'clock."

"I see it."

"You are supporting the wrong joint."

"I see it."

Kite lowered a small suction head toward the paper.

June snapped, "No."

The Reachlight paused. Its ribbon spine held the shape of attention without advancing.

Mara, at the far bench, said, "She didn't ask Kite."

"I know."

June reset the branch, found a clamp, tightened it, then discovered the washer was under the paper. She released the clamp, lifted the branch, and the pin dropped.

It struck the bench once.

Mica's old reflex fired in June's wrist: an impossible curl, a catch that could not happen.

The pin rolled into a floor seam.

Nobody spoke.

June crouched. Her hair fell into her face. She reached under the bench with five fingers and found dust, a dead moth, one of Loft's marigold coupling shims, and no pin.

Mara knelt on the other side.

"I can get another," she said.

"That isn't the point."

"No."

"Please don't agree with me like that."

Mara sat back on her heels. "Tell me which way I should agree."

Mica said, "The pin is against the left rail foot."

June's head struck the underside of the bench when she turned. "Can you not?"

Facet's iris closed halfway. "I can stop offering spatial information."

June pressed her palm to her scalp.

This was absurd. Mica was still here. Her voice was the same. Her memory of the branch was better than June's. She had not died, vanished, or forgotten. June's anger had nowhere respectable to stand.

That did not make it smaller.

"Close the shutter," June said.

Mica's iris went matte.

The effect was immediate and ugly. Mica could still hear through the collar node if she chose, still receive local workshop telemetry, still speak. But the visible act of blindness landed like a door slammed from June's side.

Mara stood. "I'm going downstairs."

"We need to finish the branch."

"We need not to finish it while all three of us are turning each other into tools."

"Kite is a tool."

Kite's lantern rotated away from them.

Mara looked up. "Bad example."

June laughed once, without humor. "You know what I mean."

"I do. I also know Kite knows when I say that about the lamp head and when I say it about the whole of him."

"Him now?"

"He hasn't objected."

"That is not a standard."

"No. It is a relationship."

June rose too quickly and the workshop tilted. She steadied herself on the bench. Her left hand spread flat against wood, shockingly simple.

Mara's expression softened. That was worse than anger.

"Go," June said. "I'll lock up."

Mara hesitated. Then she touched two fingers to the edge of the bench instead of to June.

"Show call is at nine tomorrow," she said. "You can cancel *Intervals*. The rest of the program stands."

"We are not canceling."

Mica's shutter remained closed.

Mara said, "That has to be both of you."

"It is."

"No. At the moment it is you saying plural nouns."

She left.

Below, a Waycart rolled through the loading corridor. Its reed mast rose to clear the old doorway sensor, then lowered again. A human voice thanked it. The cart answered with a wheel-hub tone too low for language and perfectly sufficient.

June found the missing pin exactly where Mica had said.

She worked until the light outside turned copper. Without Mica, every task decomposed into smaller tasks that required surfaces, clips, knees, teeth, or waiting. She had not realized how much of her work consisted of refusing to set things down.

Facet's shutter stayed closed.

At seven, June said, "Can you hear me?"

"Yes."

"Did you keep audio on?"

"No. I re-enabled when you addressed me."

June sat on the floor against the bench. "You can do that without opening."

"Yes."

"Do you want to?"

"Open the shutter?"

"See."

"I want to know whether you want me to."

"That isn't an answer."

"It is the relevant variable."

June looked at the smoky oval on the perch. "You always do that."

"Prioritize your privacy?"

"Make the clean answer sound like the only answer."

Mica opened the iris one leaf at a time.

The external camera tilted toward June, though Facet had no motor. The change was only in aperture, but June had long since learned to read it as orientation.

"I accepted the new body because I wanted it," Mica said.

There was no clean way for June to hear that.

"I know."

"You keep treating the recall as if I was persuaded by a warning notice."

"I know."

"I was not."

"I said I know."

"You say it as an accusation."

June rested her bare wrist across her knees. "I thought we'd decide together."

"So did I."

"Then why didn't we?"

"Because I was afraid your experience of losing the old body would become authority over my experience of remaining in it."

June's phantom finger tightened.

"You hid the compensation data."

"Yes."

"Because you didn't trust me."

"Because I did."

June looked up sharply.

Mica continued. "I trusted that you would understand the data. I did not trust that understanding would make the decision easier."

"That's convenient."

"It was cowardly."

The word was so unadorned that June had nothing ready for it.

Mica's iris remained open.

"I am sorry," she said. "I am not sorry I accepted."

June's hand lay palm-up. The little finger seemed stranded at the edge.

"You chose this," she said.

"Yes."

The honest answer hurt more than apology and less than a lie.

June stood. "We're done for today."

At home she set Facet on the bedside perch. Mica closed the privacy shutter before June undressed, as she always did. The familiar courtesy felt unbearable.

June woke after midnight with her left arm across her chest.

In the dark, Mica was curled around her wrist.

June could feel the old middle plate against tendon, the coral inner surface at the heel of her palm, the slight root slack Mica had been compensating out of every movement.

Her hand was empty.

Across the room, Facet's shutter was closed. A small local-status light breathed once, then went still.

June did not call her name.

* * *

The Arc-6 arrived the next morning in a case the color of wet clay.

June disliked it before Sora opened the lid.

The cuff's deep indigo had been matched from photographs, but new textile reflected light differently. The warm-bone plates were too uniform. The coral flexure had none of the tiny darkened creases that made Mica's old digit readable at a glance. Someone had transferred June's braided release loop from the recalled cuff, and its faded fibers looked obscene against everything new.

Mica said, "I asked them to move it."

June touched the braid. "Why?"

"You find it without looking."

The answer made tenderness and anger occupy the same narrow space. June withdrew her hand.

Sora fitted the cuff in stages. Echo first: dorsal bridge, ulnar strip, three haptic sites. Then the root crescent around the distal forearm. The new assembly distributed force higher, with a second textile band that crossed a part of June's skin no machine had occupied before.

"Too tight?" Sora asked.

"No."

"That was not a test of endurance."

June exhaled. "Two millimeters looser at the upper band."

Sora adjusted it.

Facet sat in the dock on the back of the cuff. Mica's iris opened, saw June's hand from its usual dorsal angle, then narrowed as the clinic linked her identity key to the new motor embodiment.

"Ready?" Sora asked Mica.

"Yes."

"June?"

"No."

Sora waited.

June looked at the three bone segments folded along her wrist. The new co-digit's proximal plate was thinner, its distal tip slightly broader. Palm had a denser tactile surface and a softer edge. The Arc track no longer exposed its mechanical spline; only a pale line beneath the service shell showed where the root could travel.

The old body had looked like something designed, repaired, and lived with. This looked like something offered.

June said, "Do it."

The new finger moved.

It did not unfold. It emerged.

The root carriage slid along Arc with almost no sound or resistance, and the proximal joint opened before June's body had finished anticipating the old friction. The sensation struck Echo too smoothly: position without effort, a finger in a dream.

June yanked her hand back.

Mica stopped outside the palm.

"I did not enter handspace," she said.

"I know."

"The root moved thirteen millimeters."

"I know."

Sora said, "Let's reduce shared-authority gain."

"No," June said. "Direct mode."

Mica's co-digit remained suspended. "June."

"I need to feel the dynamics."

"You can feel them with me stabilizing."

"I need to know what I am controlling."

Sora looked between June and Facet. "Direct mode still includes collision and force limits."

"Fine."

Mica said, "I consent to ten minutes."

June almost objected to the limit, then heard herself.

"Fine."

A calibration object rose from the table: a wooden ring, eight centimeters across, mounted on a soft post. June rested her palm around it. Sora displayed the neuromuscular channels as quiet lines.

June formed the intent pattern for Opposition.

The co-digit moved half a beat after she expected, then crossed too far. Palm struck the wooden ring and bounced.

A hot pulse marked collision through Echo.

June opened.

The digit opened.

She tried again.

This time she corrected early. Mica's root had already begun moving, and the two commands combined into a quick lateral sweep. The co-digit clipped June's little finger.

Mica stopped.

"Your predictive correction is based on Arc-4 drag," Sora said.

"I know."

"Knowing won't remove it."

June reset her hand.

Mica said, "I can compensate."

"You're not driving."

"I can modify the direct realization model without choosing the task."

"That is driving."

"It is being a motor system."

"Ten minutes," June said.

The next attempt was better. On the fifth, Palm reached the ring. June closed her natural fingers, felt artificial pressure map into Echo, and lifted.

The ring tilted.

Mica corrected automatically.

June fought the correction.

The ring shot out of the grasp.

Mica caught it.

Reflex mode moved faster than June's flinch. The root rose dorsal, the digit hooked down, and Palm trapped the wooden ring against the side of June's hand.

For one instant the old choreography returned: June dropping, Mica arriving.

Then the new root overshot by three millimeters and pressed the ring into June's knuckle hard enough to hurt.

The clutch released. The ring fell.

Nobody spoke.

June flexed her bruised finger.

Mica withdrew completely and entered Stow.

"I am sorry," she said.

"You caught it."

"I loaded you incorrectly."

"You caught it."

"Both are true."

Sora dimmed the calibration display. "Direct mode is not neutral. You are both adapting even when one of you holds task authority. If June predicts old friction and Mica predicts June's correction, the loop amplifies."

"Then disable her prediction," June said.

Mica said, "No."

The word came through the cuff speaker and through Echo: a firm pressure across the first and third sites, Mica's oldest tactile negative.

June stared at her wrist.

"I agreed to direct use," Mica said. "I did not agree to become dynamically absent."

"I am trying to learn you."

"You are trying to reproduce me."

"What is the difference?"

Mica's iris opened wider. "At present, most of the difference is twelve grams, a shorter Arc, lower tendon hysteresis, and the fact that I do not want to move as if the old carriage is still failing."

June looked away.

Sora lifted the wooden ring from the floor. "We can stop."

"No," June said.

Mica said, "Shared mode."

June almost said direct again. Her knuckle throbbed.

"Low gain."

"Agreed."

Sora reset the system.

In Shared, June provided the intent to oppose the ring. Mica selected the exact contact. The new finger began moving.

June felt herself predict the old delay. She tried not to correct.

Trying not to correct was itself a correction.

Mica paused just outside the palm.

Echo drew a small question along June's wrist: proceed?

June answered with the muscle gesture they had used for years, a brief tension in a part of her forearm that did almost nothing biologically.

Mica entered.

Palm touched wood.

June closed her fingers.

The ring remained upright.

It should have felt ordinary. Instead every contact arrived as if translated from a language June knew but had never heard in this voice.

They held the ring for three seconds.

"Release," Sora said.

June and Mica both released.

The ring stayed on its post.

"Again," June said.

Mica said, "Yes."

By noon they could grasp the ring, a cup, a folded cloth, and a broad-handled tool. They could not perform a clean self-handoff. June released too late or Mica accepted too early. Objects developed a small double motion between them, a visible uncertainty about whose grasp counted.

When they left the clinic, the older woman with Morrow was in the courtyard. She stood beside the open frame rather than inside it, eating shaved ice while Morrow held the cup at exactly the height of her elbow.

The woman looked at June's new cuff.

"First day?" she asked.

"Is it that obvious?"

"Only to people who have had one."

June nodded toward Morrow. "New frame?"

"New left support. It thinks my hip is where it was last winter."

Morrow said, "Your hip is where it is. My timing is wrong."

The woman fed it a spoonful of ice. The spoon touched a sensorless part of the frame and fell onto the grass.

Morrow looked down with its offset gaze bar.

The woman laughed so hard she had to catch the open rail.

June did not.

Outside the clinic, Mara waited beside Sunday. She looked first at June's face, then at the cuff.

"May I?" she asked.

June held out her hand.

Mara touched the upper textile band, nowhere near Palm.

Mica remained stowed.

"Hello," Mara said.

"Hello," Mica answered.

There was an ache in June's chest at the formality of it.

June flinched.

Mica froze.

Mara waited.

Mica moved.

* * *

At the Lattice, Keel and Loft had been practicing the same docking sequence for forty minutes.

Keel braced on the stage-left marks, rust-red crescent low against the black floor. Loft reversed into the open bay, sky-blue ribbon spine folded tight above the triangular base. The marigold coupling rose.

A tone sounded.

Loft rolled forward again.

"Why abort?" June asked.

Keel answered from the stage floor. "Weight arrived thirty-one milliseconds ahead of the learned window."

"Within mechanical tolerance," Loft added from across the room.

"Outside ours," Keel said.

They reset.

June watched the two robots separate and approach again. No irritation, no reassurance. Keel altered the brace angle by less than a degree. Loft reduced approach speed. The choreography was intimate only if one knew enough to see what each had changed for the other.

Mica shifted inside Stow. June felt the new root settle.

"Do not use them as a metaphor," Mica said privately through bone conduction.

"I wasn't."

"You were."

Mara, standing at the lighting desk, called, "Whenever your domestic argument has concluded."

June lifted her left hand into Kite's beam.

*Intervals* had no story. It was eight minutes of changing spaces: fingers, paper, line, light. June's biological hand created one set of shapes. Mica's co-digit created another. Sometimes they appeared to belong to one impossible anatomy. Sometimes the light separated them into two independent creatures sharing a wrist.

The audience never saw Facet, Echo, or the root track. Only shadows.

That had always been part of the pleasure. Nobody could determine which movements were direct, shared, delegated, or Mica's own. June and Mica themselves sometimes disagreed afterward.

Kite curled above the stage, lantern hood narrowing until June's hand became a dark, crisp architecture on the screen.

Mara said, "From the fifth interval."

June set her hand around a paper lattice. Mica's Palm tip positioned beneath a tension loop.

Too high.

June corrected down.

Mica corrected up.

The loop quivered. On the screen, one black line became two.

"Stop," Mara said.

June held.

Mica unloaded.

"Again," June said.

"June—"

"Again."

They reset.

In the old body, Mica had entered half a fraction before June's conscious commitment, reading neuromuscular weather and arriving where support would be needed. The Arc-6 moved faster but Mica waited longer, cautious near handspace. June had begun anticipating the caution. Their predictions circled each other like two people trying to pass in a narrow doorway.

"Direct," June said.

Mica's root stopped.

"Direct for the fifth interval."

"We agreed to shared rehearsal."

"We agreed to make a decision after testing."

"We have tested."

"And it isn't working."

Mara said quietly, "I can give you the room."

"No," June said.

Mica said, "Direct for one pass."

June took control.

The new co-digit became almost easy when Mica stopped choosing contact. June drove root, curl, and distal rotation through the learned muscle channels, while Mica handled only force and collision. The old choreography came back as an approximation. Not the sensation—never that—but the visible result.

On the screen the lattice opened. Six shadows became arches, then windows, then a hand again.

Mara let the sequence finish.

"It's clean," she said.

June lowered her arm. "There."

Mica remained in the final position until June released direct authority. Then she folded into Stow.

"It is clean," Mica said.

The distinction in her voice was slight.

Mara left the desk and came onto the stage. "It is also smaller."

"The geometry is identical."

"The geometry isn't the piece."

June looked at her. "Please don't."

"I'm telling you what I saw."

"You saw one pass."

"I have seen this piece for four years."

Mara reached toward June's wrist, intending to rotate the cuff into the light. June knew the gesture. Mica knew it too.

The new root moved.

Social Stow should have carried the co-digit outward, away from another person's skin, then curled it over the cuff. But Mica's old model initiated the wider Arc-4 path. Arc-6 reached its limit early. The distal segment compensated inward.

Palm brushed Mara's wrist.

Mara flinched.

Mica opened instantly and moved outside handspace.

"I'm sorry," she said.

June said, "It was barely contact."

Mara stepped back. "I know."

"Mica stopped."

"I know."

"No force."

"June."

"What?"

Mara held her wrist with the other hand, not because it was injured. "I know she didn't mean it. I need both of you to stop deciding that means I don't get to mind."

The stage went quiet.

Keel and Loft had stopped mid-docking. Kite's light widened, releasing June's hand from the hard-edged shadow.

Mica said, "You are right."

June's face burned. "I didn't say you couldn't mind."

"You explained why I shouldn't."

"That isn't—"

"It is."

Mara turned to Mica. "I accept your apology. Please ask before entering my contact space until the new Stow is reliable."

"Agreed."

Then Mara looked at June.

June wanted to say she was sorry. Instead she heard herself ask, "Are we rehearsing or holding a committee meeting?"

Mara's expression closed.

Mica's first and third Echo sites pressed firmly: no.

June felt the refusal as part of her own wrist.

"I withdraw consent for further performance rehearsal tonight," Mica said.

June stared down. "You don't get to—"

She stopped.

Mica did get to. They had written the co-agency covenant together. Either could end a nonessential shared task.

The knowledge was not the same as acceptance.

"We have two days," June said.

"We have tonight," Mica replied. "I am ending tonight."

June reached for the mechanical release braid.

Mica softened the digit and opened the cuff before June pulled.

That, too, was permitted. Mica could make removal easy; she could not prevent it.

June peeled Echo from her hand. The new textile left no familiar marks.

She set the cuff on the stage.

Facet stayed docked to it. Mica's iris remained open.

For a second the arrangement resembled a small creature abandoned at June's feet. June hated the image and hated herself for making it.

"You are wearing me as a prop," Mica said.

"I was controlling a finger."

"You were reproducing a result while removing the part of the result that was mine."

"The audience cannot see the difference."

"I can."

June looked toward Mara. "Is that what you think?"

Mara said, "Do not use me to outvote her."

Below the stage, Keel altered its brace angle again.

Loft reversed into the bay.

This time the coupling rose, took weight, paused, and locked.

Neither robot celebrated.

June picked up the cuff by the rigid root, careful not to touch the co-digit as if touch had suddenly become complicated.

Mica said, "You can wear me home."

It should have sounded like permission.

It sounded like distance.

* * *

June took the old cuff out of its gray tray the next morning.

The clinic had permanently de-energized the carriage and drilled a clean break through the actuation key, but they had returned the body because both June and Mica requested it. June had imagined relief.

Instead the old Kith lay on Mara's kitchen table like an object that had learned an accurate impersonation of intimacy.

The co-digit retained its shape when June lifted it. Root carriage at Stow. Middle plate a warmer bone. Coral flexure darkened in the creases. Faded braid missing from the release because it now belonged to the new cuff.

Mica watched from Facet on the windowsill. She had chosen not to be worn after the rehearsal.

June placed the old cuff around her wrist without fastening it.

The fit was exact.

The finger did not move, but June's phantom sensation rushed to meet it. For one dangerous moment sight, pressure, and memory aligned. She could almost believe Mica was curled there.

"Do not energize it," Mica said.

"I can't."

"Do not take it to an uncertified shop."

June looked toward the windowsill. "You read my messages."

"I saw the repair-cooperative appointment on the shared calendar."

"It is my calendar."

"It is labeled shared."

"Fine. I am not asking anyone to energize it."

"What are you asking?"

June did not know.

Thimble's repair room was two streets away, behind a public laundry and beneath a rooftop garden whose Rill trellis had grown almost invisible under beans. A sky-blue Glean unit spiraled down one arch as June entered, carrying a dead leaf like a flag.

Inside, Thimble waited behind the circular work hoop. Oxblood spool body, linen-colored ring, two ribbon arms wound in opposite directions. The repair robot did not look at June because it had no face. Its hooded light opened over the table.

"Place both assemblies within the hoop," it said.

June set old and new cuffs side by side. Mica's new body was empty except for low-level safety systems; Facet remained clipped to June's collar.

Thimble rotated the hoop once.

"Requested operation?"

"I don't know."

"Then I can inspect and enumerate reversible options."

"Do that."

One ribbon arm held the old cuff. The other passed a scanner beneath it. Light moved through the circular opening and made every scratch specific.

Thimble listed possibilities.

The old middle plate could be transferred cosmetically, but its mass and mounting geometry would alter Arc-6 dynamics.

The inner textile could not be reused safely; compression had permanently shaped it to June's previous load distribution.

The worn Palm surface could be made into a passive endpoint cover, but its tactile sensors were incompatible.

The coral flexure could be cut into nonstructural inserts.

The root spline could be archived intact.

The faded scratches could be copied onto the new plates.

June said, "Absolutely not."

"Copying wear is available and reversible until finishing."

"It is fake."

"Yes."

Mica said, "Transfer nothing structural."

June's left hand closed on the edge of the hoop. "I heard you."

Thimble's ribbons withdrew from both cuffs. It had reached an irreversible-action pause despite not being near an irreversible action. Perhaps it read tone better than sentiment.

"What do you want from it?" Mica asked.

June looked at the old body.

She wanted the pressure pattern back. The tiny root slack. The way Mica's distal segment settled against her wrist at night. The old social Stow. The old catch. The old half-fraction in which Mica moved before June understood she had needed movement.

Nothing Thimble could transfer was any of that.

"I want something to remain," June said.

Mica's voice softened, though the words did not. "I remain."

"Not in my hand."

"No."

The admission occupied the room.

Outside, Glean reached the bottom of the trellis and stopped. Rill lowered a cream docking ring by three centimeters, turning it slightly to meet the crawler's weakened left clamp. Glean entered on the second try. The garden did not applaud.

Thimble said, "The release braid has already been transferred."

June glanced at the new cuff. "Because I find it without looking."

"That is a functional continuity," Thimble said.

"Don't turn this into a lesson."

"I was classifying the modification."

Mica made a small sound through Facet. Not laughter exactly, but the pressure pattern June would have felt if Echo were attached.

June looked at the old middle plate again. "Remove it."

Mica's iris narrowed. "Why?"

"Not for the new body. Just remove it."

Thimble held the cuff inside the hoop. "The plate is non-load-bearing after de-energization. Removal is reversible."

"Do it."

The ribbon arms released six tiny fasteners. The warm plate lifted free.

Without it, the old co-digit looked opened, ordinary, a set of dead tendons and service channels.

June's stomach turned.

Thimble placed the plate in her palm.

It weighed almost nothing.

Mica said, "June."

"I know."

"What will you do with it?"

"I don't know."

"Then keep it until you do."

They left the rest intact.

Back at the Lattice, Mara was painting repair lines onto a translucent screen. Kite held the sheet upright and placed light along the brush edge. Mara looked at the plate in June's hand and did not ask.

June clipped Facet into the new cuff.

"Do you consent to wear?" Mica asked.

"Yes."

"Do you consent to shared calibration without rehearsal?"

June swallowed the reflexive argument. "Yes."

"Do you consent to Mara being present?"

June looked at Mara.

Mara set down the brush. "I consent to being present. I don't consent to contact."

Mica said, "Agreed."

June fastened Echo and the upper band.

They began with no object.

June opened her palm. Mica unloaded the root, moved to the edge of handspace, and waited.

The question traveled along Echo.

June answered.

Mica entered.

No grasp. No goal. She simply occupied Opposition, the new Palm hovering over June's lifeline.

June's body predicted old friction. She let the prediction occur without obeying it.

Mica's root held.

After ten seconds, June released.

Mica withdrew.

Again.

On the sixth pass, June stopped anticipating the old endpoint and began noticing the new one.

On the tenth, Mica came too early. June tensed; Mica withdrew before contact.

On the twelfth, June's answer gesture misfired and the co-digit remained outside. They both waited, then started over.

"This is not enough for Friday," June said.

"No," Mica agreed.

"Do you want to cancel?"

Mica's iris opened fully.

"I want to perform something," she said. "I do not want to impersonate the old performance."

Mara leaned against the bench. "That is also what I want."

June looked at the paper city, the tension branches, the screens already marked for *Intervals*.

"What would we perform?"

Mica moved to the edge of handspace and waited.

June realized the answer was not a title.

She gave the gesture.

Mica entered.

They practiced the threshold.

For an hour they did almost nothing worth watching. Approach. Ask. Answer. Contact. Yield. Re-enter. June's natural fingers moved around the artificial one instead of commanding it through memorized shapes. Mica held positions without predicting the next useful position. They allowed pauses large enough to see.

The old middle plate sat on the bench inside Kite's light.

At some point Mara came close enough to hand June a cup.

She stopped outside the reach of the new co-digit.

"May I?" she asked.

June felt Mica's root unload.

"Yes," June said.

Mica moved outward, not into full Stow. June rotated her wrist a little to widen the path.

Mara put the cup into June's natural fingers.

No one touched anyone else.

It felt, for the first time that week, less like failure than a rule they might learn to live inside.

That night Mara found June on the old baths roof, where Rill's solar leaf folded against the dark.

The garden was closed, but the building never locked the roof to residents. Glean slept in the docking ring, a blue spool among leaves. Beyond the parapet, the city moved through several kinds of companionship: low carts beside walkers, canopies blooming at crossings, one Morrow frame waiting open outside a late café while its person finished an argument inside.

June had removed the new cuff. Mica's Facet sat on the bench between them, shutter open to the sky.

Mara lowered herself beside June.

"I am sorry," June said.

"For which part?"

"Explaining your own wrist to you."

"Good choice."

"And using you to settle an argument with Mica."

"Also good."

June turned the warm middle plate over in her hand. "Do you think she is separate from me?"

Mara considered. "I think I am separate from you."

"That wasn't the question."

"It is the answer I have."

June looked at her.

Mara continued. "When the two of you agree, you become very easy to treat as one person. Efficient. Elegant. A little insufferable."

Facet's iris narrowed.

Mara touched the bench, not June. "When you disagree, everyone around you has to remember the seam. You hate that because the seam runs through your skin."

"It doesn't run through my skin."

"No. It runs through your decisions. Worse location."

June rubbed the old plate with her thumb.

"I don't know how to be angry at her without feeling angry at my own hand," she said.

Mica spoke from the bench. "I do not know how to leave an argument without leaving your body."

The garden made small nighttime sounds around them: irrigation ticking, leaves folding, the quiet adjustment of Rill's structure under wind.

Mara said, "That sounds inconvenient."

"It is," June and Mica said together.

This time Mara did smile.

June's laugh came late and cracked in the middle.

Mara waited until it was finished. Then she asked, "May I hold your hand?"

June looked at her bare left hand.

"Not that one," she said.

Mara took the right.

Mica closed her shutter, not because June had asked, but because that was the agreement they had made years ago.

For the first time, June wondered what closing it cost.

* * *

On Friday evening, the city reached forty-one degrees and stayed there after sunset.

Vela canopies opened along the plaza outside the Lattice, cobalt petals showing saffron undersides. The building's Choir system lowered entry temperatures room by room rather than pretending the old stone could be cooled all at once. Waycarts arrived with water, folding stools, instrument cases, garment bags, and one enormous paper moon that had to be tipped sideways through the door.

The program was called *Bodies in Common*.

June had objected to the title six months earlier. Mara had said this was because June objected to every title that admitted a theme. Mica had said it was because the phrase could mean almost anything and therefore probably would.

Now the words appeared on the old tile above the entrance, illuminated by a ceiling carrier that had been stuck in place since afternoon.

June and Mica were fourth.

Backstage, Keel braced beneath Loft. The pair had completed every docking attempt that day, though June noticed Keel still paused three seconds longer than before the coupling locked. Loft's sky-blue spine rose into the rigging grid, drawing tension through the translucent screens.

Kite waited on the overhead rail with his lantern closed.

Mara checked the projection marks.

"You can still stop," she said.

June flexed her hand. The new Kith was warm against her wrist, not overheating, simply present. Mica had been quiet for twenty minutes, conserving both battery and language.

"Do you want to?" June asked.

Mica answered through Echo first: a slow pressure at the center site, then the first. Their agreed signal for *uncertain, leaning yes*.

Aloud she said, "I want to begin."

"That is not the same."

"No."

June looked through the wing gap. The previous act was ending: a Hush unit and three dancers creating pockets of directional sound that moved through the audience. People turned their heads as whispers arrived beside them without crossing the room. Onstage, Lilt's lavender gills gathered, leaving the dancers exposed in ordinary light.

Applause began unevenly, then found itself.

June said, "I want to begin too."

Mara touched the edge of June's right sleeve. "Then begin. You don't owe them the old piece."

The audience did not know there had been an old piece.

That was the last private cruelty of it.

The stage manager raised two fingers.

Kite moved.

His ribbon spine uncurled above the stage and placed a single warm plane of light across the first screen. June walked into it with her left hand held low.

The paper city waited on a narrow stand: black branches, hinged roofs, tension lines almost invisible. The warm middle plate from Mica's old body sat in June's pocket. She had not decided why she brought it.

The light found her hand.

Five natural fingers and one artificial arc became one black shape on the screen.

The first interval had always begun with Mica arriving first.

June's thumb and forefinger approached a paper ring. The co-digit moved toward its underside.

At the edge of handspace, Mica waited.

The pause was visible.

June gave the answer gesture.

Mica entered.

Palm touched paper. June closed. Together they lifted the first roofline.

On the screen, the city rose.

The movement was slower than before. The audience could see the negotiation. June hated that for the first ten seconds, then had no attention left to spend on hating.

The second interval used Loop. Mica held a tension line mechanically while June's fingers walked two paper figures through a narrow gate. The old choreography had made the line seem to float. The new one showed the co-digit taking weight, root carriage traveling, June's wrist turning to accommodate.

A child in the second row whispered, "It's helping."

Another voice whispered, "No, it's doing the other one."

June almost smiled.

Third interval.

Mica moved Overhand to hold a small translucent disc above June's knuckles. Kite shifted color temperature. The disc became a moon; June's natural fingers became birds moving across it.

The new root was too quiet.

June missed the moment of arrival and rotated early. The moon tilted. Mica corrected. June corrected against her.

The disc spun.

A line snapped.

On the screen, the moon fell through the city.

The audience made the small collective sound people make before deciding whether an error belongs to the work.

June froze.

Mica's distal segment had caught the broken line. The paper disc hung below their hand, turning slowly.

In the old piece, the moon did not fall.

In the old body, Mica would have felt the line beginning to fray through the tactile surface and shifted load before it broke.

Perhaps.

June's mind supplied the old competence as if nothing had ever failed before.

Kite held the light.

Mara did not cut it.

Echo pressed once at June's center site.

*Stop?*

June could lower her hand. The screens could go dark. The audience would applaud kindly or not. Nobody would die. Nobody would even lose much money. The stakes were small enough to reveal exactly how much of them belonged to pride.

June gave the signal for continue.

Mica did not move.

A different pattern crossed Echo: first site, third, center.

*Delegate?*

The request opened a hollow in June's chest.

Delegated control meant Mica would choose and maintain the task inside the agreed workspace. June could move her natural hand independently. They had used it thousands of times—hold this wire, keep the bowl stable, maintain tension—but not since the replacement. Not in performance. Not with a broken line and another person's wrist only meters away in the wing.

June opened the autonomy gate.

Mica took the falling moon.

Not by restoring it.

The co-digit curled around the broken line and lowered the disc beneath June's palm until it became a second shadow, suspended under the hand. Root carriage moved dorsal. Palm rotated. The moon hung where a sixth finger could not naturally hold it.

June released every muscle command associated with Mica.

The artificial digit remained.

Her hand felt suddenly divided. Not amputated. Plural.

She moved her biological fingers around the held line.

A bird crossed the fallen moon.

Mica tilted the disc.

The bird became a fish.

Somebody laughed—not mockery, surprise—and the sound changed what June did next. She opened her fingers into water. Mica moved the moon through them, not as a finger now but as an independent object-handler sharing the same wrist.

June's skin crawled with the unfamiliar haptic map. She knew where Mica was without controlling her. The knowledge was intimate and insufficient.

The fourth interval had been architecture. June abandoned it.

She lifted the paper branch from the stand. Without the snapped line, one side sagged. Mica caught the lower joint. June raised the upper. The city unfolded between two intentions.

They were not smooth.

Mica moved too far left. June followed instead of correcting. June turned faster than Mica expected; Mica released rather than resist. The branch collapsed, reopened, and became an animal shape nobody had designed.

June felt the next request at the edge of handspace.

She answered.

Mica entered early.

June waited.

The movement did not oscillate.

It propagated.

Hand to digit, digit to paper, paper to light, light to screen. Each part arrived before the next and after the last. Not command. Not imitation. A sequence with no stable first cause once it was underway.

The old middle plate pressed against June's thigh inside her pocket. It had no role.

Fifth interval.

Mara changed Kite's angle. The first screen went dim and the rear screen brightened. June and Mica's shadows separated.

June's natural hand appeared on one surface.

Mica's co-digit, raised Overhand, appeared on the other.

For years, this had been the trick at the center of *Intervals*: one body split into two silhouettes, then reunited. The timing depended on the old Arc's wide travel. Arc-6 could not reach the same geometry.

June had planned to fake it by turning her shoulder.

Instead Mica moved the root to its new limit and stopped.

The artificial shadow fell short.

June stepped sideways.

Her own shadow crossed the gap.

For an instant the five-fingered hand overlapped the single curved digit. Not one hand. Not two. A shape whose anatomy changed depending on which screen the eye trusted.

June opened and closed her fingers.

Mica did not mirror.

She made a small separate motion, distal tip rotating toward June's shadow.

June answered with her little finger.

The two shadows touched.

There was nothing technically difficult about the gesture.

It was the first time all week June did not know whether Mica had anticipated her or she had anticipated Mica.

The last interval approached.

In the old piece, Mica caught the paper city as June released it. The entire structure hung from the sixth finger while June's natural hand withdrew from the light. The audience saw an impossible hand holding a city alone.

It was the image people remembered.

It was also fourteen newtons on the root under lateral load.

The recalled assembly had held it three nights before Mica accepted replacement.

June looked at the city.

Mica knew where she was looking. Facet was open on the cuff; her external viewpoint saw both the object and June's hand.

"We do not need it," Mica said privately.

The words entered through bone conduction, too quiet for the room.

June thought of the raw compensation trace. The thorn hedge. Mica holding the south line and deciding, while June worked, that she wanted another body.

"We don't," June whispered.

The audience could not hear.

June lifted the city with both biological hands.

Mica remained in Stow.

For the first time in any performance, June used her right hand in Kite's light.

Two ordinary hands held the structure.

Then Mica unfolded—not to take the weight, but to open one small roof that June could not reach without releasing something else.

The city expanded.

Keel and Loft raised the rear screen.

Mara widened Kite's beam.

The paper branches cast roofs, antlers, roots, ribs. June held most of the load. Mica held one hinged square no larger than a postage stamp.

That square changed the whole shadow.

June felt the contact through Echo: pressure, position, a tiny vibration from paper.

Mica sent no request.

June sent no command.

At the final cue, they released together.

The city folded onto the stand.

Darkness came before applause.

June stood with her arm extended into nothing.

Her hand shook.

Mica moved to Stow but did not complete the curl. The root paused halfway along the new track.

June rotated her wrist outward, giving her the missing room.

The co-digit settled around the cuff.

The applause began scattered, then dense, then complicated by the fact that the next act's scenery had already started moving. June heard it and did not know what to do with it.

Mara met them in the wing.

"Moon line," she said.

"Snapped," June replied.

"I noticed."

Mica said, "The south anchor had abraded the filament."

Mara looked at June. "You changed the last interval."

"Yes."

"Good."

June almost demanded a more elaborate judgment. Mara was already signaling Keel to clear the paper stand.

The world had not paused to certify what had happened.

June stepped aside.

The Vela canopies outside had closed into cobalt buds. Choir dimmed the corridors floor by floor. Somewhere above, Rill began its night irrigation, water ticking through the old roof pipes.

Mara sat beside her.

Kite curled along the rail overhead, lantern hood closed. Keel remained braced while Loft undocked, the marigold coupling lowering between them. The two robots paused after separation, recalibrating the loads they no longer shared.

"I forgot it was there," she said.

"No."

"I do not know."

"Did that feel like you?" June asked.

"The performance?"

"The new body."

"Yes."

"I know."

"If you moved the same, I wouldn't have to decide whether you were still you."

Mica's co-digit did not move.

"Did you decide?" she asked.

"No."

"Neither did I."

That was not reassurance. June felt something in herself unclench around it anyway.

Mara shifted closer. "May I touch your left hand?"

The question was directed at June.

Then she added, "Either of you can say no."

June opened her palm.

Mica unloaded the root.

The social Stow path began outward. On the old body it had been one fluid motion, invisible from the inside. On Arc-6, the root reached its shorter limit and paused.

June felt Mica calculate.

She could have waited. She could have told Mara not yet. She could have taken direct control and completed the old path herself.

Instead June rotated her wrist, opening the angle between cuff and palm.

Mica curled through the space June made.

The movement was not the old movement. It did not need to pretend.

Mara placed her fingers in June's bare palm.

June closed her natural hand around them.

Mica stayed folded along the outside, present as warm weight and artificial position. Through Echo, three small pressures crossed June's wrist: center, first, third.

A pattern they had used before either of them could remember which one invented it.

June answered with a muscle gesture that moved almost nothing.

Mica's distal joint settled one millimeter.

Mara's thumb traced the side of June's little finger. "All right?"

June listened to her own hand.

"Not yet," she said.

Mara nodded.

They sat there anyway.

After a while, Keel rolled away from Loft. Kite extinguished the last work light. The old baths settled around them, pipes and stone giving up the day's heat.

Mica moved.

June moved.

For once, neither arrived first.
